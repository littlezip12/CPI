What WPI measures before Junior Olympics

Pre-JO rankings are useful, but they are not final. This note explains how WPI treats verified results, opponent quality, bracket path, recency, and team identity before JO results are reconciled.

Pre-JO rankingsMethodology noteNo ranking changes

The short version

WPI is designed to organize available tournament evidence. It is not a popularity poll, and it is not a single-event bracket table. Before Junior Olympics, the model is intentionally conservative because team aliases, division paths, and roster-depth questions still need review.

What counts most right now

The strongest signals are verified tournament results against known opponents. A result gets more useful when WPI can understand the division level, the bracket path, the quality of the opponent, and whether the team is an A, B, C, or mixed roster entry.

Recency matters, but it does not erase the full season. A strong recent run can move a team, but one weekend should not automatically wipe out months of connected results.

What WPI is still cleaning up

The largest open cleanup areas are team aliases, club identity, team-depth labels, missing logos, and post-JO placement reconciliation. That is why these rankings should be read as a Pre-JO snapshot rather than a final season grade.

What happens after JO

The post-JO update will compare each team’s Pre-JO WPI rank with its actual JO finish, tournament path, division context, and prior body of work. The goal is not only to update the rankings, but also to learn where the model was right, where it missed, and which aliases or team-depth assumptions need correction.

Pre-JO WPI is a ranking system with context. It is not a replacement for JO finish, and it will be recalibrated once JO results are available.
